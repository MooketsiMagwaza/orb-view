# Idea

## One sentence

Orb View is a quiet, spatial interface for moving through the connected ideas that make up a mind.

## The premise

Thought rarely behaves like a folder tree or a chat transcript. One idea leads sideways into another: entropy leads to heat, engines, industry, silicon, memory, culture, mortality, and nature. Orb View gives those relationships a place to exist visually.

The product begins with a few large concepts rather than attempting to be a complete encyclopedia. Each concept reflects a recurring curiosity:

- how matter becomes technology;
- how cultures, religions, and emotions change;
- how humans cooperate and contradict themselves;
- how nature creates order inside constant change;
- how machines constrain energy into useful work;
- how philosophical ideas affect ordinary choices.

## Why entropy is the root

Entropy connects the physical and personal parts of the network. It can describe energy spreading, structures decaying, time having a direction, or people creating temporary islands of order.

From entropy, the first visible branches are eight entrances, chosen to cover the physical, the made, the living, and the meant:

- **Culture** — stories, religion, language, music, history, ritual, and emotion changing across generations.
- **Humanity** — consciousness, identity, cooperation, conflict, care, and contradiction.
- **Nature** — cycles, ecosystems, life, the planet, decay, renewal, and peace.
- **Cosmos** — the universe, stars, gravity, light, black holes, and its far future.
- **Mathematics** — numbers, proof, chance, symmetry, and the structures behind everything else.
- **Silicon** — stone refined through extreme precision into wafers, transistors, and RAM.
- **Computing** — software, data, networks, security, and the infrastructure they run on.
- **Machines** — engines, clocks, mechanisms, electricity, and intelligence.

These are entrances, not final categories. Their descendants link back to one another, and the deepest of them, truth, meaning, existence, and nothing, are reached from many directions.

Beyond exploring freely, the network offers **paths**: short, ordered trails through it, grouped as *Me*, *Tech*, *People*, *Science*, *Culture*, and *Abstract*, for learning one idea at a time.

## What makes it different

Orb View is neither a chatbot nor a static mind map.

- The network is explored directly instead of through a text command.
- A tap continues the journey; a hold pauses it for reflection.
- The interface remembers spatial relationships rather than presenting a message history.
- Motion and sound communicate state without filling the screen with controls.
- Explanations are short, personal, and concept-led rather than formatted like assistant replies.

## Product principles

1. **Let curiosity lead.** A person should be able to follow an interesting connection without deciding on a destination first.
2. **Keep the canvas alive.** The graph should drift and breathe, but remain readable and stable enough to learn.
3. **Focus without losing place.** Holding a node should create calm without destroying the surrounding context.
4. **Reveal complexity gradually.** Only nearby relationships appear at first; deeper detail unfolds through exploration.
5. **One family, many forms.** Every concept is a dotted blue orb, and each wears the one of nine `thinking-orbs` forms whose motion suits it (see [ORBS.md](ORBS.md)). Shared color, size, and style keep it a single language; the form, the tempo, and the writing give each idea its personality.
6. **Treat difficult subjects responsibly.** Religion, weapons, conflict, and identity are described with context, care, and appropriate safety boundaries.

## Not part of the core experience

- A visible grid or diagram editor
- A conventional chat transcript
- A free-text input field
- Persistent cards showing previously opened explanations
- A menu of disconnected encyclopedia categories (paths are trails *through* the network, not separate lists)
- Endless text: the note stays short, and depth lives one deliberate step away in the info sheet
